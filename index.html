What cyclists ask us before they call

What should I do right after a bicycle accident?
  • Call 911 and get checked by a doctor, even if you feel fine.
  • Photograph the vehicle, your bike, your injuries, and the scene.
  • Get the driver's insurance details and contact information for witnesses.
  • Keep your damaged bike, helmet, and clothing. Do not repair or throw anything away.
  • Do not give a recorded statement to the driver's insurer before speaking with a lawyer.
Can I still recover if I was partly at fault or not wearing a helmet?

Often, yes. Mississippi reduces your recovery by your share of fault. Tennessee and Arkansas allow recovery as long as you were less than 50 percent at fault. Helmet use can affect how damages are argued, so talk to a lawyer before assuming your claim is lost.

How long do I have to file a bicycle accident claim?

Deadlines vary by state. Tennessee generally allows one year for personal injury claims, while Arkansas and Mississippi generally allow three years. Camera footage and witness memories disappear far sooner, so contact a lawyer as early as you can.

What if the driver fled the scene or has no insurance?

You may still have a claim. Uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage on your own auto policy, or a household member's policy, can often apply even though you were on a bicycle. We review every policy that might respond.

What compensation can an injured cyclist recover?

A bicycle accident claim can include current and future medical bills, lost income and reduced earning capacity, replacement of your bike and gear, and compensation for pain, suffering, and lost quality of life.
